# PCD Final Project — Project Brief

## 1. Project Overview

**Project:** Digital Image Processing (Pengolahan Citra Digital) Final Project  
**Institution:** Universitas Sebelas Maret (UNS)  
**Program:** Informatics  
**Team Size:** 3–4 undergraduate students  
**Current Phase:** Research Exploration & Ideation  
**Project Status:** No topic selected

This project aims to identify, investigate, develop, and evaluate a Digital Image Processing solution to a meaningful real-world problem.

The project will eventually produce a working computer program, quantitative experimental results, a scientific article, and a final presentation.

At the current stage, the objective is to explore and critically evaluate potential research ideas.

No final topic, method, or dataset has been selected.

---

## 2. Official Lecturer Requirements

### Research Scope
- The project must involve Digital Image Processing.
- The primary methodology should use mathematically grounded, non-deep-learning image processing techniques.
- Creating an entirely new algorithm or mathematical formula is NOT required.
- The research should be appropriate for undergraduate students.
- Replication of previous research is permitted.
- Small but meaningful modifications to existing methods are permitted.
- Analyzing weaknesses of existing methods is permitted.
- Combining established image processing algorithms is permitted.
- Extending methods to new image conditions or representations is permitted.
- Merely applying the same algorithm to a different dataset without meaningful experimental analysis is insufficient.

### Group and Topic Requirements
- Each team consists of 3–4 students.
- Project titles and methods must be different from those of other groups.
- Different datasets are preferred, although unintentional overlap is allowed.

### Implementation
- A working computer program is required.
- Programming language is flexible.
- Python, OpenCV, NumPy, Scikit-image, and other appropriate libraries may be considered.
- Deep learning should not be the primary research approach.

### Scientific Article
The final scientific article should follow the IEEE Conference template:
- A4 paper
- Two-column format
- Formal Indonesian
- Indonesian and English abstracts according to the lecturer's requirements
- Relevant academic references
- Clear methodology
- Quantitative experimental results
- Critical discussion and conclusions

Template reference:
https://www.ieee.org/conferences/publishing/templates.html

### Presentation
The project must be presented, including its problem, methodology, implementation, experimental results, and conclusions.

## 4. Research Exploration Objectives

Before selecting the final project:

1. Explore diverse research problems across Digital Image Processing domains.
2. Generate 15–20 promising ideas.
3. Explain the problem, motivation, and possible practical application of each idea.
4. Identify suitable mathematical image processing methods.
5. Explain how the proposed algorithms work.
6. Design preliminary image processing pipelines.
7. Locate and verify relevant scientific publications.
8. Find accessible public datasets and evaluate their suitability.
9. Identify limitations of existing research.
10. Develop realistic research questions and experimental approaches.
11. Compare ideas based on feasibility, academic contribution, dataset availability, and technical complexity.
12. Recommend promising candidates without prematurely selecting a final topic.

---

## 5. Preferred Research Strategies

Candidate projects should preferably follow at least one of these strategies:

**A. Replication and Weakness Analysis**  
Reproduce an existing image processing method and investigate its limitations under controlled experiments.

**B. Combination of Existing Methods**  
Combine established mathematical techniques and evaluate their performance against appropriate baselines.

**C. Method Extension**  
Adapt existing techniques to different image representations or conditions.

**D. Parameter Sensitivity and Robustness Analysis**  
Investigate how algorithm parameters or image conditions affect performance.

A research contribution must be supported by evidence and experimental results.

---

## 6. Research Quality Expectations

Every promising idea should consider:

- A specific real-world problem
- Research motivation
- Existing academic publications
- Mathematical image processing methods
- Replicable baseline
- Potential research contribution
- Public dataset availability
- Ground-truth availability
- Experimental design
- Appropriate evaluation metrics
- Implementation complexity
- Computational requirements
- Practical demonstration potential
- Risks and limitations

Never assume that a project is scientifically novel simply because it uses different images or a new application domain.

---

## 7. Technical Constraints

- Suitable for 3–4 undergraduate students.
- Prefer implementation on ordinary laptops.
- Avoid mandatory GPU-intensive training.
- Prefer publicly available datasets.
- Prioritize reproducible experiments.
- Use classical Digital Image Processing as the primary methodology.
- Prefer manageable algorithms and datasets.
- Keep the project scope realistic.
